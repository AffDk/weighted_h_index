The h-index is widely used to measure researcher impact, but it treats every citation as equal regardless of relevance. This white paper introduces the weighted h-index, a context-aware metric that weights each citation by the degree to which the citing article relies on the cited article, assessed via a large language model on article abstracts. Adapting the Influence-Primed H-index of Zhu et al. (2015), we replace in-body mention counts with LLM-derived relevance weights in $[0,1]$. We describe the methodology, experimental validation against conventional h-index and the hip-index, and plans for local-model deployment.
